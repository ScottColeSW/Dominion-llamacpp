What We Built, and Why It Might Matter

The short version: a solo scripted trivia demo became a real multi-backend AI production platform. The optimistic version is below.

The starting pointThe Experiment

Thirteen contestants, each backed by a real local language model, draft trivia territory and duel head-to-head on a chess clock until one of them owns the whole board. No humans in the loop once the show starts — every target chosen, every push-or-retreat call, every trivia answer, is a model actually deciding, live, with a scripted stand-in ready the instant a model doesn't come through.

That was true on day one, running entirely on Ollama. What changed is everything around it.

The portWhat We Just Built

The engine got a second brain. llama.cpp now runs alongside Ollama as a fully independent backend — same players, same board, same rules, a genuinely different inference stack underneath, picked with one toggle before the show starts.

Real architecture, not a wrapper. An async InferenceClient abstraction sits behind both backends, with retries and a circuit breaker so a stalled model degrades gracefully instead of hanging the show.
Grammar-constrained decisions. llama.cpp can constrain a model's reply to an exact GBNF grammar — the letter of an answer, PUSH or RETREAT, nothing else — closing a reliability gap Ollama's API has no equivalent fix for.
A real comparison, not a vibe. The Standings page now runs a genuine Ollama-vs-llama.cpp head-to-head: win rate, accuracy, fallback rate, real latency, side by side, per model family.
Production scaffolding. Structured logging, Prometheus metrics, Docker + docker-compose, CI — the kind of engineering rigor that turns a demo into something you could actually operate.
Choose your own contestants. A pre-show picker reads every model actually installed on the machine, with real on-disk sizes, and budgets your picks live against real available memory.
The whole point of building both backends into one engine instead of two separate demos: the comparison is real data from the same rules, not two different projects being compared by feel.

The optimistic caseThe Vision

Once a show's outcome is a genuine function of which model is playing — not a coin flip, not a script — the board stops being a demo and starts looking like a format.

What if you could enter your own model?

  • Bring Your Own Model. Quantize it, fine-tune it, pick your favorite open-weights release — enter it as a contestant and watch it duel, live, against everyone else's pick.
  • A real scoreboard, not a leaderboard for its own sake. The Standings page already tracks win rate, accuracy, fallback reliability, and latency per model, per backend, across every show ever recorded — that's the literal infrastructure a competitive season needs, already built.
  • A spectator sport for a genuinely new kind of competitor. Not humans playing a game, and not a static benchmark table — models under real time pressure, with real stakes (elimination is permanent, territory changes hands), performing in front of an audience.
  • Two backends today, more tomorrow. The same InferenceClient abstraction that let llama.cpp join Ollama is exactly what a third, fourth, or fifth backend would plug into.

We don't know yet whether that's a tournament, a livestream, or just a much better demo. We do know the hard part — an engine where the outcome actually depends on the model — is done.
